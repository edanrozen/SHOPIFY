# DOFEK knee brace · animated film

Hebrew vertical commercial for the knee brace, built on Higgsfield.
Two cuts delivered: 31 s and 16 s, 1080x1920, H.264 + AAC.

## Concept

One man, two locations, before and after. The same supermarket aisle and the
same park path are shot twice from the same angle, so the change reads without
narration. He is in shorts in every shot, so the brace is visible throughout the
second half.

Beats: morning pain, stopped mid aisle, left behind in the park, orders on the
phone, courier at the door, fits the brace, turns the dial, walks the aisle
without stopping, leads his family, end card.

## Product accuracy

The brace was never described from memory. Every shot that shows it was built
from the merchant's own photographs passed as image references, and the two
close ups reproduce the dial, the lace loops, the perforated pad, the grey
branching knit pattern and the DOFEK label exactly. The end card is the real
product photograph, not a render.

## Production notes

Every keyframe was animated with kling4_0 in image to video mode, 5 s, 1080p,
9:16, the keyframe passed as start_image. The first pass asked for a locked off
camera to protect product fidelity; that protected the product and made the film
read as moving stills. The second pass specified a camera move per shot (dolly
in, lateral track, drift past, push in over the shoulder, low orbit, backward
dolly, orbit) and that is what shipped. Product fidelity held.

Hebrew captions are never generated by the model. They are rendered separately
in Chromium at 1080x1920 with transparency, in the store's own typefaces
(Rubik and Assistant), and composited with ffmpeg. This is why there are no
spelling errors. The same applies to the end card.

Music is an original instrumental generated with elevenlabs_music_v2_5,
34 s, no vocals, cleared for commercial use.

Cost: roughly 500 Higgsfield credits for the whole production including
retakes and one duplicate run during a provider stall.

## Reuse

To advertise another product with the same man, keep the two character masters
and generate new keyframes from them with the new product's photographs as
additional references. The look, the face and the wardrobe stay consistent
across the brand.
